Five questions for every citation

Each answer is stored with its reason and the source response it rests on. The first failure, in this order, becomes the citation's status.

  1. Does the case exist? One lookup resolves every cite in the filing, then a search by name, court and year, then the RECAP docket. A cite to a reporter series that doesn't exist fails on its own.
  2. Does the cite point to it? The case at the cited volume and page has to carry the name the filing gives it.
  3. Are the quoted words in the opinion? Each quoted passage is matched against the opinion text, allowing for OCR slips, brackets and ellipses.
  4. Does the opinion support the statement? Claude reads passages the code picked from the opinion and nothing else from the filing but the one sentence.
  5. Is the pincite inside the opinion? Checked against the reporter's page breaks. A bad page is flagged next to the status.

It works inside a budget. CourtListener allows 125 calls a day. At the limit Gainsay pauses, shows when the quota refills, and resumes from its ledger without repeating a single settled check.

It recovers. Dropped connections are retried. A crash or an outage mid-run resumes from the last recorded finding.

It shows its work. While a run is active, its page says in one sentence what it just did and what it does next, and every attempt is a neatlogs trace.
